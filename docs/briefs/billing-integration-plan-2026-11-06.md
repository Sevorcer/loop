## 1. Where we are

- LOOP bills nothing. "Ready to Bill" is a checklist: a finished job waits for someone to type a QuickBooks invoice number.
- The office keys every invoice into QuickBooks by hand (no Service Fusion to QuickBooks sync today).
- `invoices` (2,370 rows) and `payments` (1,966) exist only as **imported Service Fusion history**. `invoices` has `number`, `total_cents`, and a boolean `is_paid`. It has no status, no balance, no line items, no due date, and no QuickBooks id. `payments` has amount, date, type and reference.
- `job_line_items` (5,875 rows) holds imported priced lines (service / product / other_charge).

## 2. What Service Fusion actually does for you (from the real account)

1. Invoice number is generated by Service Fusion (#8290, 11/03/2026).
2. Three independent states: job status (stayed "Unscheduled"), invoice (number/date), payment (method Direct Bill, terms Due Upon Receipt, **Unpaid, $400.00 balance**).
3. Builder rules live in **pinned free-text notes** on the parent account: PO required, net-30 / pay by check, copy estimators and PMs on invoices. Nothing enforces them (this invoice says Due Upon Receipt against a net-30 note).
4. Sub-customers (one per job site) invoice to the parent builder.
5. Builder change orders and billing events are separate dummy jobs ("Bill out ...", "Change order ...").

## 3. Principles

- QuickBooks stays the system of record for the books. LOOP owns the operational invoice (what, for whom, how much, when, status). We do not rebuild accounting.
- Money in LOOP is integer cents. Money tables are append-only where they record events (payments), same standard as the inventory ledger.
- Every phase ships useful on its own and can stop there.
- No third-party credentials are stored in the repo. Tokens live in encrypted server-side storage and are never sent to the browser.
- Each external call is idempotent (client request id on every write) and every sync attempt is logged.

## 4. Phased plan

### Phase 0: Decisions and facts (no code)
Needs Collin plus whoever does the books. See section 6. Nothing below starts until questions 1 to 4 are answered.

### Phase 1: LOOP invoices (native, no external calls)
- New `invoices` columns or a new `billing_invoices` table (decided in the Phase 1 plan; extending the imported table risks mixing history with live data). Fields: status (draft / sent / partial / paid / void), issue date, due date, terms, PO number, balance (derived), QuickBooks id (nullable), source (`sf_import` | `loop`).
- Invoice lines copied from the job (labor, parts, reconciled materials from Lane 4), editable on the draft.
- LOOP-generated invoice numbers in a gapless per-org sequence, with a configurable prefix and starting value that matches QuickBooks so numbers never collide.
- Builder account fields (replaces pinned notes): PO required, default terms, invoice copy-list contacts, parent billing account.
- Payments recorded against an invoice, append-only; balance and status derive from them.
- Ready to Bill becomes "create invoice" instead of "type a number"; the empty-state and completed-date bugs in note #26 get fixed here.
- Imported Service Fusion invoices and payments surface read-only on the job and customer pages.

### Phase 2: Change orders and draws (builder work)
- Change orders and draws are line items or child items of the parent job and bill without a dummy job.
- Progress billing: bill a percent or fixed amount of a job in stages, with a running billed-vs-contract total.
- This replaces the Service Fusion "Bill out ..." job pattern. Karl's builder spreadsheet and the GC ledger link here.

### Phase 3: QuickBooks hand-off
Pick one after question 1 (Online vs Desktop). Recommended order:

- **3a. Export file** (any QuickBooks version): CSV (Online import) or IIF (Desktop) of invoices and payments, with a "not yet exported" queue, a batch id, and an exported flag so nothing exports twice. Low risk and quick to build.
- **3b. Direct sync (QuickBooks Online only)**: OAuth connection, then push customers, invoices, and payments; map each LOOP line to a QuickBooks item and income account; store the QuickBooks ids; reconcile nightly and surface mismatches. Needs a mapping screen, retry/dead-letter handling, and a rule for edits after sync (LOOP edits a synced invoice only through a credit/replacement, never silently).
- Customer matching is done once, explicitly, with a review screen. No fuzzy auto-creation of QuickBooks customers.

### Phase 4: Payment processing
Depends on question 3 (which processor you use and want to keep).
- Pay link on the invoice and emailed invoices (customer pays by card or ACH).
- Webhook receiver verifies the processor signature, writes a payment row idempotently, and updates the invoice. Refunds and failed payments are recorded as separate events, never edits.
- Deposits and card reader (field collection) are later, only if you want them.
- The processor's own fees and payout reports map to a QuickBooks deposit; we never handle card numbers (processor-hosted pages only), which keeps us out of card-data scope.

### Phase 5: Cut-over
- Run LOOP invoices alongside Service Fusion for a trial period on a few accounts, compare totals with QuickBooks weekly, then retire Service Fusion billing.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Invoice numbers collide with QuickBooks or Service Fusion history | Set the LOOP sequence start above the highest existing number; confirm with the bookkeeper before first live invoice |
| Double-posting to QuickBooks | Idempotency keys, stored QuickBooks ids, exported flag; sync only from a single place |
| Imported history mixed with live invoices | Separate `source`, history stays read-only |
| Tax and class/income-account mapping wrong | Mapping screen plus a dry-run report before any live push |
| Webhooks replayed or forged | Signature check, event id uniqueness |
| Sensitive tokens leak | Server-only encrypted storage, never in client bundles or logs |
