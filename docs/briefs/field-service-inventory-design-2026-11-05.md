# LOOP Can Catch Missing Install Parts

**LOOP can give Legacy One Heating something no surveyed field service platform was shown to offer: a pre-dispatch readiness check that compares a job's required materials against warehouse and van stock and flags shortages before the pallet leaves the shop.** ServiceTitan, BuildOps, Successware, FieldPulse and Workiz already cover the basics: warehouse and truck locations, min/max reorder points, and mobile consumption logging. ServiceTitan also reserves stock ("On Hold") and generates requisitions from sold estimates. None of the documentation reviewed describes an automated required-versus-available shortage check at dispatch time, but that is an inference from absence in thinly sourced notes and needs a second look before it goes in a pitch. Reviewers' loudest complaints point at the same design: technicians cannot see their own truck stock, consumed parts never get logged back so counts drift, and purchase-order receiving breaks when suppliers substitute. LOOP starts from zero: the audit found no inventory tables in any migration, a read-only list that reads an unmigrated table, and no vehicles table. That is an advantage, because the ledger-first design below can be built correctly once. The recommendation is a transactions-ledger model with locations (warehouse and vans), a serialized-unit table, install-type kit templates that expand into per-job material plans, a readiness check, a mandatory materials reconciliation at job close, and purchasing deferred to a late, deliberately lightweight phase. Evidence is thinner than the decision deserves (Reddit and G2 were unreachable, and several platforms had no usable reviews), and no authoritative install bill of materials (BOM) was found, so every kit in this report is a proposal to validate with Collin.

## Competitors converge on trucks-as-locations, but nobody clearly closes the pre-dispatch loop

The common model across the platforms with real inventory is that a truck is a location with a parent warehouse and an assigned technician. ServiceTitan's Inventory Locations page has separate Trucks and Warehouses tabs, and each truck shows its warehouse, assigned techs, stock template and per-item quantities split into Available, On Hold, On Order, On Hand and Total ([ServiceTitan locations](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/inventory-locations-overview)). Successware treats any stock-holding place as a "warehouse," including a truck identified by plate or unit number ([Successware](https://www.successware.com/blog/2023/june/did-you-know-inventory-manager/)). FieldPulse lets a truck be its own "hub" ([FieldPulse](https://help.fieldpulse.com/using-fieldpulse/item-list-inventory.md)). Workiz sells inventory as an add-on with warehouse, office and vehicle locations ([Workiz](https://help.workiz.com/hc/en-us/articles/18055926157585)). Housecall Pro, Jobber and FieldEdge appear to lean on the third-party app Ply or on QuickBooks for truck stock ([Housecall Pro/Ply](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/10431432-ply-inventory-management-integration-overview), [Jobber community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/operations-forum/inventory-management/4964), [Ply/FieldEdge](https://help.getply.com/en/articles/16204663-using-ply-inventory-in-your-fieldedge-workflow)). Two caveats: the notes were produced from sub-model summaries of fetched pages, so detail may be lossy, and no pricing or tier gating was verified for any vendor. ServiceTrade and Fieldwire were not covered at all.

On kits and dispatch checks specifically, the evidence is weakest. ServiceTitan's pricebook-linked materials and sold-estimate requisitions are the only documented path from a job definition to a material list, and its requisition screen filters for "on hold" items and shows "Quantity Available to Pick," but the fetched documentation did not describe shortage flagging ([ServiceTitan requisitions](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/manage-install-requisitions)). Workiz markets parts "assigned to jobs so technicians arrive prepared" without mechanism detail ([Workiz](https://www.workiz.com/features/inventory-management/)), and BuildOps expects the tech to check the truck and request restock ([BuildOps](https://buildops.com/features/purchasing-inventory)). The honest reading is that reservations exist, shortage detection at dispatch was not shown, and the gap could be real or could be undocumented. Vendor integrations are a second axis LOOP should note and defer: ServiceTitan has procurement integrations with Johnstone, Gensco, Consolidated and others, with Ferguson and Trane/American Standard still partial ([ServiceTitan suppliers](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/available-supplier-integrations)). No Seattle-specific supplier workflow was verified.

## Reviewers want tech-visible trucks, enforced check-in and forgiving POs, but the evidence is thin

Confidence caveat first. Reddit, HVAC-Talk, G2 body text and Trustpilot were not retrievable. Workiz, BuildOps and Successware had no usable user evidence, and recent FieldEdge reviews were absent. What remains is dozens of data points, skewed toward ServiceTitan's own community forum (mostly 2022-2024), plus a Jobber community thread and Capterra reviews for Housecall Pro, Service Fusion and FieldPulse. "Frequency" below means independent sources seen, not a rate. The ServiceTitan product may also have changed since those posts.

The best-evidenced gripe, with four or more independent posts, is that technicians cannot see their own truck stock, min/max or pending replenishments in ServiceTitan; one responder said the company hands each tech an expected-stock spreadsheet instead ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/Can-Technicians-view-their-Truck-Inventory/m-p/46118), [ST Community Feb 2024](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/Technician-Truck-Inventory/m-p/45472)). The most concrete one is count drift: a service company reported that filters bought by techs on POs inflate truck counts because consumption is never logged back, causing missing parts, double buying and delays, and asked for either a required post-use check-in or a way to buy job-specific parts without touching warehouse counts. A June 2024 reply found no workaround ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/Inventory-effected-by-Purchase-Orders-Inventory-Management/m-p/48621)). Third, PO receiving is clunky when suppliers substitute: with auto-receive, a change means deleting the receipt, moving the PO back to pending, editing and receiving again, and once bills are batched they cannot be edited ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/Can-someone-explain-service-titans-thinking-on-the-purchsing/m-p/31001)). Cycle counts were described as paper-heavy or Excel-driven ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/How-does-your-company-manage-inventory/m-p/12625)). At the low end, a 2026 Housecall Pro reviewer reports no inventory tracking ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140363/housecall-pro/reviews)) and a Jobber owner could not track parts across technicians' trucks and two storage areas ([Jobber community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/operations-forum/inventory-management/4964)). A Service Fusion founder with 10,000+ parts across three locations built a workaround and wanted transfer tracking and restock trends ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/135788/Service-Fusion/reviews/)). Low-confidence items, such as a comparison blog's "24% of complaints" claim about Housecall Pro, are excluded from the design reasoning. No user evidence at all was found on serial-number tracking complaints or warranty-registration wishes.

Praise is sparse and tilts toward integration rather than depth: emailing POs straight to vendors (Service Fusion, Jan 2026), a flexible price book (Housecall Pro, FieldPulse), and cost visibility per job. One ServiceTitan user cited auto-replenishment and tech recording of parts used from trucks as working strengths ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Inventory/Can-Technicians-view-their-Truck-Inventory/m-p/46118)). Workarounds tell the same story: owners use Sortly QR bins, Ply, Zapier plus Monday, pre-stocked truck bins and expected-stock sheets. Reading the pattern, the wishes that recur and map cleanly to design are tech-visible truck stock with min/max and replenishment status, mandatory post-job parts check-in, job-specific buys that bypass stock, editable receipts with an audit trail, scan-based counts, and supplier price feeds. A small contractor-focused platform that ships those few things well would differentiate against mid-tier tools described as lacking them.

## Install kitting practice is real, but item-level BOMs are a proposal

**No authoritative, published item-level install BOM by job type was found.** What exists is advice: ServiceTitan users link materials to equipment or build pricebook kits and print the estimate as the pull sheet, while noting that checklist forms handle variable quantities poorly and that a free-text materials email to the warehouse was a poor process ([ST Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Project-Tracking/Materials-list-for-equipment-installations/m-p/19228)). Large replacement contractors build a job ticket listing equipment, zoning, controls and components, send purchasing a copy early to confirm stock, and stage all job materials the day before in a zone assigned to that installer near the warehouse doors ([ACHR News](https://achrnews.com/articles/127104-my-two-cents-failing-to-plan-is-planning-to-fail)). HVAC School's phase-based checklist adds a night-before proposal review, a check that the equipment box is complete, and a before-leaving-the-shop pass over consumables such as wire nuts, brazing alloy, PVC primer and glue, and large items such as line sets, breakers and surge protectors ([HVAC School](https://www.hvacrschool.com/how-to-eliminate-surprises-on-install-jobs/)). Line-set kits are sold by length and tube size, which illustrates the size-variant structure a BOM needs ([HVACStore](https://hvacstore.com/products/air-conditioning-installation-kit-30-3-8-x-7-8-tdckit001)). Distributor-side failure modes are worth encoding: main equipment arriving without controls or accessories, substituted models, direct-to-site shipments wrongly added to warehouse stock, and one backordered accessory blocking several jobs ([Stacksync, vendor](https://www.stacksync.com/blog/hvac-distributor-edi-for-mechanical-contractors-track-equipment-pos-backorders-and-partial-receipts)).

The proposed BOM schema is my synthesis, not a sourced standard: per install type, lines tagged required, conditional (a rule on survey answers such as attic versus crawlspace, or reusing an existing line set) or optional upsell, with size selectors for tonnage, line-set diameter and length, breaker amperage and flue diameter. Because the survey drives variants, the BOM is a draft at scheduling that the installer confirms. Collin's own install history is the right source for quantities. The Service Fusion import already holds free-text job and estimate line items with cost, and those names can seed a first BOM draft for him to correct (the audit confirms they carry no SKU). Nothing found covers Pacific Northwest specifics such as water-heater seismic strapping or Washington code items.

On par levels, the consensus is min as the restock trigger and max as the reload ceiling, built from roughly 12 months of per-truck usage, reviewed quarterly. Contractor case studies range from identical loadouts with a supplier-managed buffer (10 trucks), through trucks modeled as inventory locations that draw down by invoice and restock from usage reports (23 trucks), to minimal truck stock that leans on local parts houses (40 trucks) ([ACHR News](https://achrnews.com/articles/140977-top-four-strategies-to-manage-hvac-fleet-inventory)). Vendor blogs supply numbers, such as a $3,000-8,000 parts target per service truck and a reorder point of daily demand times lead time plus safety stock, but they are service-oriented and undisclosed in method ([Pipeline](https://pipelineon.com/blog/truck-stocking-list/), [SimPRO](https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/hvac-inventory-management-best-practices)). For an install company, the implication is a split: a small consumable par on each install van replenished by min/max, and job-specific materials issued from the shop through the job kit rather than carried on the van. Kit-workflow claims (accuracy rising from 80-85% to 93-97%, morning staging dropping from 35 to about 5 minutes) come from a vendor page the notes flag as internally inconsistent and modeled; treat them as directional only ([Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/hvac/hvac-parts-kit-assembly-pre-staging)). One useful practice from that source is a warehouse "missing/unneeded" flag at verification that feeds back into the kit. For refrigerant, DOT rules exempt up to 440 lb combined (A1 or A2L) from placarding, and A2L cylinders generally ride upright ([Arkema](https://forane.arkema.com/en/resources/tech-tips/transporting-a2ls-in-service-vans/)). That suggests tracking cylinders as serial-like units by location and keeping R-454B and R-410A as separate SKUs, but Washington storage rules and current EPA dates were not verified.

## LOOP starts from a blank ledger with useful stubs

Every finding here is code-verified at repo HEAD bceacd9, with no live database queried. The `/inventory` route renders a read-only list that calls `/api/inventory`, which selects from an `inventory_items` table that **exists in no migration**; the repository swallows the missing-table error and returns an empty list, and the provider hard-codes empty allocations and material plans. Create, adjust, transfer, reserve, pick and load are all missing, as are any service layer, `CoreTable` entry or RLS. The API route piggybacks on the `jobs` select permission, and navigation limits the screen to owner, manager, dispatch and office, so techs cannot open it today. There are no vehicles, locations, vendors, purchase orders or pricebook tables. The only truck concepts are the free-text `vehicle_alerts.vehicle_name` and `crews.truck_name`. A Sprint 18 domain model already exists in `src/features/inventory/types/inventory.ts` (items, allocations, `JobMaterialPlan`, `MaterialReadinessState`, warehouse job cards) with pure readiness-derivation helpers. Dispatch has stubbed hooks for it: a material-readiness input type, an `awaiting_materials` status, and a seed that hard-codes readiness as `not_satisfied`.

Existing data to build on: `installed_systems` holds `serial_numbers text[]` and accessories and is required by "full" job closeout; `jobs.type` is a CHECK of Install, Service, Maintenance, Inspection, Estimate, Callback with **no install sub-type, no job-template table and no bundle table** (`equipment_bundle_id` is an opaque string); `estimate_line_items` and `job_line_items` carry kind, name, qty and `cost_cents` but no SKU. `job_tasks` already seeds an install checklist template, which is the nearest thing to a template concept. The project constitution frames inventory as the material-readiness domain that "should inform operations without swallowing purchasing or accounting scope prematurely," which argues for phasing purchasing last. Two conventions need care. First, the current `InventoryProvider` is a client component fetching in `useEffect`, contrary to AGENTS.md's server-first rule, so the rebuild should replace it. Second, migration hygiene has drifted: recent verify files sit in `supabase/migrations/`, the checksums file stops around 20261114, and recent migrations lack `-- DOWN` sections, so `verify-migrations.sh` checks 5 and 7 likely fail on main (inferred; the script was not run). New work should follow the documented standard and flag the drift. The next safe migration version is **20261121000000**, which sorts strictly after 20261120800000.

## The proposed twist: a ledger, a kit expander and a readiness gate

### Data model

Stock truth lives in an append-only ledger; quantities per location are a trigger-maintained cache, which gives auditability and makes the reversals competitors handle ad hoc (cancelled jobs, deleted invoices) a matter of posting an opposite entry. All tables carry `org_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES organizations(id)`, money in `*_cents`, and the newest RLS pattern from the audit.

| Table | Purpose and key design choices |
|---|---|
| `vehicles` | Real van entity (name, plate, status, assigned user or crew). Replaces free-text `truck_name` and `vehicle_name` over time via nullable FKs |
| `inventory_locations` | Type enum: warehouse, van, job (virtual, for buy-for-job), quarantine/RMA; optional parent warehouse, optional `vehicle_id` |
| `inventory_items` | Catalog: SKU, name, category, unit, tracking mode (quantity or serialized), brand, model, manufacturer part number, `is_active`, size attributes (tonnage, diameter). Seed with Legacy One brands |
| `inventory_item_costs` | Cost and preferred vendor split into a separate table so techs and portal users cannot read cost; mirrors the existing rule that `cost_cents` is sensitive |
| `inventory_stock_levels` | Item and location: on hand, min, max (par); reserved is derived from material plan lines; written only by ledger trigger except min/max |
| `inventory_serial_units` | One row per furnace, heat pump, coil, air handler, mini-split or refrigerant cylinder: serial, item, status (in stock, reserved, staged, loaded, installed, returned, RMA), location, `job_id`, later `installed_system_id`, warranty fields |
| `inventory_transactions` | Ledger: type (receive, transfer_out/in as a paired entry, issue_to_job, return_from_job, adjust, count, buy_for_job), item, serial unit, from/to location, qty, job, user, reason, reversed-by link |
| `install_types` and `kit_templates` | Install types such as furnace swap, AC/heat pump, mini-split, coil/air handler, full changeout, water heater; each template has parameters (tonnage, line-set length, breaker amp) |
| `kit_template_lines` | Item or item group, base qty, quantity rule, condition (required, conditional on a parameter or survey answer, optional) and variant map for size selectors |
| `job_material_plans` and `_lines` | Expansion of a template per job: planned qty, status (planned, picked, staged, loaded, used, returned), source (kit, manual, estimate), confirmed-by installer, shortage note |
| `pallets` (or `pull_lists`) | Staging unit per job: lines scanned or tapped onto it, packer, packed-at, installer-verified-at, "missing/unneeded" flags that feed back to the kit template |
| `vendors`, `purchase_orders`, `purchase_order_lines`, `receipts` | Late phase: PO lines may be linked to a job and flagged buy-for-job; receipts support partial quantities, substitutions and edits with audit history |

Because `jobs.type` has no sub-type, the install type must live on the job (a nullable `install_type_id`) or on the plan; this is the first open question.

### How the flows work

Scheduling an Install job expands its template into a material plan, with the installer or estimator confirming conditional lines from survey answers. **The readiness check** then compares each plan line to what is physically available: warehouse stock plus the assigned van's stock, minus quantities already committed to earlier jobs, and for serialized lines whether a unit is assigned and actually received. The result reuses LOOP's existing `MaterialReadinessState` vocabulary and feeds the dispatch fields already stubbed (`materialReadiness`, `awaiting_materials`), so dispatchers see "short: 2 whips, 1 condensate pump" the day before rather than at 7 a.m. The pallet flow follows the staging practice found in trade sources: pull list generated, items scanned or tapped onto the job pallet, installer verification before departure, and a missing or unneeded tag that improves the template. A van restock is a pair of transfer entries with Successware's two-step confirmation (warehouse sends, tech confirms received), and replenishment suggestions are created only for jobs that have started, following ServiceTitan's cancelled-job lesson. At close, a **mandatory materials reconciliation** step blocks "full" closeout until every plan line is resolved as used, returned to van or returned to warehouse, with extras added by the tech. Serial units used on the job link to `installed_systems.serial_numbers`. This is the direct answer to the ServiceTitan count-drift complaint. Reorder points are per item per location; a low-stock alert fires when available quantity falls below min, with the suggested quantity equal to max minus available, grouped by preferred vendor in the manner of FieldPulse's auto-drafted POs. The design deliberately keeps one source of truth, so there are no manual adjustments outside the ledger, which avoids the double-counting seen with one-way syncs.

### Differentiators versus competitors

Four ideas carry the product. A dispatch-time readiness check (not shown in any surveyed source). Tech-visible truck stock with min/max, last restock and pending transfers, which ServiceTitan users repeatedly asked for. A mandatory check-in at close, which a ServiceTitan user requested and could not get. And buy-for-job POs that land in a virtual job location and bypass stock levels, so ad hoc supply-house purchases neither inflate van counts nor skip cost tracking. Receipts stay editable with history, since substitution handling was the PO-side pain point.

### Roles and permissions

Roles are resolved from `user_profiles.app_role`, never from user metadata. The mapping below follows the existing patterns (`job_bids`, `vehicle_alerts`, `job_permits`) and is a proposal for Collin to confirm.

| Area | owner | manager | office | dispatch | tech | sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catalog, locations, par levels | write | write | write | read | read (own van's items) | read |
| Costs and vendors | write | write | write | none | none | none |
| Stock levels and ledger | read/adjust | read/adjust | read/adjust | read | read own van and own jobs | none |
| Transfers and van restock | all | all | all | request | confirm receipt on own van | none |
| Kit templates | write | write | write | read | read | read |
| Job material plans and readiness | write | write | write | read, override with reason | read/write on assigned jobs (via `job_assignees`) | read |
| Materials-used logging at close | override | override | edit after close | read | write on assigned jobs | none |
| Purchase orders and receiving | all | all | all | read | create buy-for-job request only | none |
| Delete | owner | owner | owner | none | none | none |

Cost hiding is enforced by table separation (`inventory_item_costs`) rather than by UI alone. Portal users are denied everywhere, as in the existing portal model.

## A five-lane build plan that respects LOOP's guardrails

AGENTS.md requires an implementation plan, explanation of files and architecture, and **approval before code**, then `npm run build` verification. Each lane below is therefore a plan to approve, not a green light. Each migration needs a version after 20261120800000 (starting at 20261121000000, each strictly later), a companion `supabase/verifications/<same name>.verify.sql`, a `-- DOWN` section, a checksum entry via `scripts/update-migration-checksums.sh`, `ENABLE` plus `FORCE` row-level security, policies named "table: description" scoped `TO authenticated` with `current_app_role() IN (...) AND org_id = current_org_id()`, no `WITH CHECK (true)`, `fn_set_updated_at` triggers and idempotent DDL. Every new table needs a `CoreTable` entry in `src/services/authorization.ts`, with the same commit updating `rls-role-matrix.md`, `authorization.test.ts` and `endpoint-authz-inventory.md`. The inventory API must stop borrowing the `jobs` permission. Pages stay tiny (`page.tsx` renders a screen from `src/features/inventory`), routes go in `src/lib/routes.ts`, colors use tokens only, and the client `InventoryProvider` is replaced by server components with small client islands for scanning and forms.

| Lane | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | `vehicles`, `inventory_locations`, `inventory_items`, `inventory_item_costs`, `inventory_stock_levels`, `inventory_transactions` with the stock-cache trigger; service layer and write routes; catalog and stock screens; seed brands and the baseline catalog from Collin's data | First migration 20261121000000; resolves the missing-table state and replaces the empty list; fix migration-verify drift or flag it explicitly in the PR |

Metrics to track from lane 4 onward, drawn from the install-specific list in the notes and not from sourced benchmarks: kit completeness at dispatch, missing-part events per install, supply-house runs on install day, over-pull and return rate, and parts-attributed callbacks. No independently verified benchmark exists to compare against.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the problem. Competitors already supply locations, min/max and mobile logging, and what reviewers complain about is the loop around them: techs cannot see their truck, consumption is not enforced at close, and purchasing exceptions corrupt counts. LOOP can win by closing those loops with a ledger, a required close-out reconciliation and a buy-for-job path, and by running a readiness check before dispatch. Because the evidence for the pre-dispatch gap is an inference from sparse documentation, the check should be treated as a hypothesis to prove with Legacy One's own near-misses before it is positioned as unique.

The largest uncertainty is not technical but the kit content: with no published BOM to anchor to, the quality of readiness detection depends entirely on how accurately the first two or three install templates reflect what Legacy One's crews actually need. Starting narrow, running readiness in advisory mode and using the pallet "missing/unneeded" tags to improve templates turns every missed item into training data for the system.
